CSP Inspector Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 26, 2026

In short: CSP Inspector does not collect, store on any server, sell or share any data. Everything it reads stays in your browser and is deleted when you close the tab or the browser.

What the extension does

CSP Inspector is a developer tool. It shows the Content-Security-Policy (CSP) of the web page in your current tab, lists requests that the policy blocked, and lets you test whether a URL would be allowed.

Information the extension reads

To do this, the extension reads the following, only on pages you visit:

  • The Content-Security-Policy and Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only response headers of the page, and any CSP <meta> tags in it.
  • CSP violation reports the browser raises on the page. These include the address of the page, the blocked address, the script file and line that triggered the violation and, for blocked inline code, the short sample (up to 40 characters) that the browser includes in the report.
  • The address of the current tab, to show which page the policy belongs to.

Apart from that sample, it does not read page content, form input, passwords, cookies, browsing history or any personal information.

How the information is stored

The headers and violation reports are kept in the browser's temporary session storage (chrome.storage.session), separately for each tab. They are removed when the tab is closed, when the page is fully reloaded, when you click Clear, or when the browser is closed. Nothing is written to disk by the extension and nothing is synced to your Google account.

Information sharing

The extension has no server and makes no network requests of its own, except one: when you open the popup and the policy was not captured during the page load (or you click Refetch), it requests the current page again from that same website to read its headers. This request goes only to the site you are already visiting.

No data is sent to the developer or to any third party. There is no analytics, advertising or tracking.
